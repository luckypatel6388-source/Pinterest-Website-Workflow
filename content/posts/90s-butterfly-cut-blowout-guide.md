A butterfly blowout is built on layers that sweep away from the face, with a little lift at the roots. If you want to try the 90s version at home, three tools do most of the work: a hot air brush to dry and shape, a heat protectant spray to prep, and jumbo velcro rollers to set the shape. Here is what each one does, who it suits and what to keep in mind.

## What to look for

- **Brush shape:** an oval brush has flatter faces for smoothing and rounded edges for lifting at the roots, which fits the sweeping layers of a butterfly cut.
- **More than one setting:** look for several heat and speed settings, so you can start gentle and adjust as you go.
- **A prep spray for hot tools:** check the label for where and how to apply it, and for the heat range it lists.
- **Roller size:** jumbo rollers make wide, soft bends rather than tight curls, which is the shape a butterfly cut calls for.

## Wash-day tips

- Start with damp hair, not dripping wet. Towel-dry first so the brush has less to do.
- Mist your prep spray from mid-lengths to ends, then detangle before you start.
- Work in small sections. Pull the brush up and away from the face at the root, then turn the ends outward to sweep the layers.
- Wrap each warm section around a jumbo roller, leave it until it has cooled, then unwind it gently.

## Common questions

**What is a butterfly blowout?**
It is a layered blowout where the face-framing layers sweep outward and away from the face, with lift at the crown. It works best on hair that already has layers.

**Do I need all three tools?**
No. The brush dries and shapes, the spray is a prep step, and the rollers are an extra way to set lift at the crown. Choose the ones that fit your routine.

**Can I use velcro rollers on their own?**
Yes. They are heatless, so they can be used without a hot tool.
